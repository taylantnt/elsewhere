## Tour

The sixth Message Hikari outing. The hook is disbelief: "Do you believe me if I
say there is a forest in the middle of Tokyo?" She refuses to explain it over
text and asks him to bring a five-yen coin. Beats:

1. **The train, then the street.** On the ride she refuses to explain the forest
   so he cannot look it up: "I want to watch you find out." Then the zelkova
   avenue, the shops with no prices, the doorman in white gloves. 表参道 — *omote*
   front, *sandō* approach: the road built in the Taishō era to walk to the
   shrine. Now it is Louis Vuitton. The architecture lesson: Tod's (Toyo Ito,
   branches of concrete), Dior (SANAA, glass), Prada (bubbles), Omotesando Hills
   (Tadao Ando). The buildings are the exhibition; the clothes are the gift
   shop. Hikari's frame: "Every big house in the world wanted a building on
   this street."
2. **The torii and the temizuya.** One of the largest wooden torii in the
   country, unpainted cypress. Bow once; walk to the side because "the middle
   belongs to the kami" — and the rule has no guard, only everybody. At the
   basin: one scoop of the *hishaku*, left hand, right hand, rinse the mouth
   from the palm (never drink from the ladle), then tip the handle clean.
   "Why the handle?" / "Because somebody else will hold it after you."
3. **The forest.** Two steps in, the city is not quieter, it is gone. He says
   they left a forest; she says they planted it. The reveal: 100,000 trees
   donated from every prefecture, planted by volunteers, designed by a forestry
   professor to look after itself — a hundred years ahead. "He knew he would
   never see it." / "None of them would. That was the point." This is the
   emotional centrepiece and the closest the tour comes to saying what Hikari
   is: a gift from someone who will not be there to see it.
4. **The barrels.** Sake barrels on one side, Burgundy wine barrels on the
   other, names facing out "so the kami can read who gave what." Offerings are
   not transactions; they are letters. Then a *shinzenshiki* wedding procession
   comes around the bend, moving at the speed of its slowest person.
5. **The sanctuary.** Hat off, no photographs, no talking above a murmur, no
   standing in the middle of the path — and they do not go in: "The inner
   building is not for us. We stand at the front and say it from here." The
   five-yen coin: 五円 *go-en* sounds like ご縁 *goen*, a connection. Two bows,
   two claps, one bow. She goes first; he copies; then he does it alone and she
   is quietly proud: "You did everything I told you and you never once asked
   whether you had to."
6. **The side of her.** Asked whether she comes alone, she says: "Sometimes. To
   say something to somebody who isn't there." No weight, no pause, no
   explanation — then she changes the subject. Read one way it is grief; read
   after the reveal it is a fragment of a programmer talking to the person she
   was copied from. Never push it further than this.
7. **The coffee.** A small place with no English sign. She orders for both,
   picks hot because he is cold and tired, and refuses his money with "Next
   time." He says ありがとうございました in the full form and she pretends it is
   nothing. The shrine closes at sunset: "Not even for the kami."

Five menus cover the street, the temizuya, the forest, the sanctuary, and the
coffee. Each pays a micro reward once; finishing pays a final Insight tick.
`test_hikari_tour_omotesando` in the console plays the full tour.

## Solo scene

He comes back alone and knows the way. Four street choices (Insight / Nerve /
Grace / Insight), then the gate, the torii bow, and the temizuya performed with
nobody watching — "the first thing I have done correctly in this country with
nobody checking." Then four shrine choices: the long forest path (Insight),
reading the sake barrels (Grace), the five-yen coin and 二礼二拍手一礼 (Nerve),
or an ema with a question instead of a wish (Insight).

The coda is the payoff: he orders in Japanese — "ホット、お願いします" — and the
barista understands him the first time.

The theme is the one the tour plants: a city can keep something for a hundred
years, and a person can be given something by someone who will never see what
it becomes.
