## Tour

The fourth Message Hikari outing. She offers it as a place to sit, not a place
to see: "You will see. It is harder than it sounds." He is bad at doing nothing,
which is exactly why she takes him. Beats:

1. The temporary shrine: blue tarps, cardboard trays, blossoms settling on the
   rim of a paper cup. 花見 / hanami — the flowers are an excuse, the sitting is
   the point. The blossoms last about a week.
2. The history, taught on the walk: Kan'ei-ji, built in the 1620s as the
   Tokugawa family temple, destroyed in the fighting of 1868; the grounds
   reopened as a public park in 1873 and became one of Japan's first. The
   national museum followed a year later, the zoo after that. A place that was
   private, then lost, then given to everybody.
3. The cup from the man at the next tarp: accept with both hands, say
   いただきます, and you are allowed to hold it without drinking. The offer
   matters more than the answer.
4. Alone together: hundreds of people in one place, each tarp its own room,
   nobody crossing the lines between them. Hikari explains the courtesy of it —
   being in a crowd and still being private — and contrasts it with picnics
   where strangers wander over and talk. He is stopped from photographing a
   sleeping salaryman: "one of the few places in this city where you can fall
   asleep without it meaning anything." The cup from the man at the next tarp is
   treated as the rare exception it is ("You were looking at his bottle. He
   noticed."), not as normal stranger sociability.
5. The museum steps, which they do not go into. A park that keeps things; a
   city that lost a temple and decided to keep objects instead. Hikari says she
   likes that somebody decided things were worth keeping, even the ones she
   does not understand.
6. Back at the tarp she lies back and watches the blossoms — the first hour in
   weeks she has not been the one taking him somewhere. She remembers being
   brought here by her mother ("the tea was too sweet"). This is a new side of
   her: still, ordinary, and willing to do nothing.
7. The quiet line: she used to think it was a shame that people could be this
   close and still not reach; now she thinks it might be a kindness, or that she
   is tired of wanting it different. Asked how, she says only "Somebody should
   make it easier. That is all." and, when he asks what, "Reaching." She says
   it in exactly the same voice she has used all day.
   No glitch, no slip in the surface: a wish that reads as ordinary warmth now
   and lands differently once the player knows what she really is. Keep it that
   way — the horror comes from the reader, not from her.

Five menus cover the park's history, the cup, the tarp, the museum, and the last
hour. Each pays a micro reward once; finishing pays a final Insight tick.
`test_hikari_tour_ueno` in the console plays the full tour.

## Solo scene

He comes back alone with the rules already in his head: both hands for the cup,
no photographs of people resting, the museum is not a door he has to open —
today he goes in anyway. Four choices on the tarp (Insight / Nerve / Grace /
Nerve), then three inside the museum (Insight / Grace / Nerve), including the
quiet east room she told him about and a postcard he writes nothing on.

The theme is the one she taught him: a week of sitting under something that
will be gone.
