ELSEWHERE — MASTER CHARACTER ART PROMPT

Create a premium-quality character illustration for Elsewhere, a cinematic visual novel set in modern Japan.

1. Art Direction and Visual Style

Use a sophisticated 2.5D semi-realistic anime illustration style.

The artwork should combine:

High-end mature anime character design.

Semi-realistic facial proportions and anatomy.

Beautifully rendered skin with subtle natural texture, soft gradients, and realistic shading.

Highly detailed hair with individual strands, natural volume, and realistic highlights.

Detailed fabric rendering with believable folds, stitching, material textures, and clothing construction.

Expressive, carefully illustrated eyes with realistic reflections.

Cinematic lighting with a warm key light, subtle cool fill light, and delicate rim lighting.

Professional illustration quality comparable to premium modern Japanese visual novels.

The result should feel like a carefully painted 2.5D character render rather than a flat anime drawing, generic AI anime artwork, or photorealistic photograph.

3. Anatomy and Proportions

Maintain anatomically believable human proportions.

Pay particular attention to:

Natural shoulder, neck, and torso proportions.

Correct arm and leg anatomy.

Realistic hand structure and finger positioning.

Natural facial symmetry without making faces artificially perfect.

Believable weight distribution and posture.

Correct interaction between hands and objects.

Hands must be carefully rendered, with five anatomically correct fingers whenever visible.

Avoid malformed anatomy, extra fingers, missing fingers, fused limbs, distorted joints, and unnatural poses.

4. Clothing and Materials

Render clothing with realistic fabric behaviour.

Show natural folds, tension, seams, layering, stitching, and material differences.

Clothing should feel physically believable and appropriate for the character's personality, occupation, and environment.

Avoid overly decorative details unless specifically requested.

Character should be clearly separated from the background.

Maintain a clean silhouette suitable for use as a visual novel sprite.

7. Background and Transparency — CRITICAL

The final output MUST have a genuinely transparent background.

Technical requirements:

PNG format.

RGBA colour mode.

A genuine alpha channel.

Fully transparent background pixels (alpha = 0).

Fully opaque character pixels where appropriate (alpha = 255).

Smooth, clean antialiased edges with proper alpha transparency.

Preserve fine hair strands and delicate clothing edges.

ABSOLUTELY NO:

Black backgrounds.

White backgrounds.

Coloured backgrounds.

Gradient backgrounds.

Environmental backgrounds.

Checkerboard patterns.

Simulated transparency.

Background lighting effects or atmospheric halos.

Shadows extending into the transparent area.

Do not draw a checkerboard to represent transparency. The background must actually be transparent in the exported PNG.

8. Final Quality Requirements

The final character should look like a professionally produced premium visual novel asset.

Prioritize:

Character identity and consistency.

Anatomical accuracy.

High-quality facial rendering.

Detailed hair and clothing.

Natural poses and expressions.

Cinematic 2.5D rendering.

Clean silhouette and genuine RGBA transparency.

The final image must be suitable for direct integration into a Ren'Py visual novel project.

3. Important rules for our game assets


Consistent rendering

Every character should look like they belong to the same game, even when their ages, nationalities, occupations, and personalities differ.










Anatomical quality

Hands, faces, clothing, and props deserve particular attention. No rushed or malformed details.











Character individuality

Avoid repetitive faces, hairstyles, body types, and generic expressions. Characters should feel like actual people inhabiting modern Japan.

One particularly important technical distinction: Image generation doesn't always produce genuine transparency, even when requested. We should verify the exported PNG's RGBA mode and alpha channel rather than trusting a checkerboard preview or a prompt claiming transparency.
