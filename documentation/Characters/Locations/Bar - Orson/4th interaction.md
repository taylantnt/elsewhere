[Slow night. Rain taps the window. Orson pours two modest servings and keeps one.]
Orson:“I used to be a “normal” person. Dress shoes, morning trains, meetings that used words like ‘synergy’ with a straight face. I was good at it. That was the problem.”
Player: “So… why did you leave that life behind?”
Orson: “Because, being good at something and being alive inside it are different things.”
(swirls his glass)
Orson: “I wanted change. I wanted… not to count years by fiscal quarters. I failed some expectations. Then I succeeded at failing them.”
Orson: ”Also I learned that the only constant in life is change. Whether it's good or bad is up to you.”
(a smile in his voice)
Orson: “This place is the result. A third space. Not home, not work, it's a place where you can exhale.”
Player: “I’m still figuring out where I fit.”
Orson (nods): “That’s honest. Here’s a Tokyo trick: choose a few ‘anchors’. A coffee shop you claim. A park bench you check in with weekly. A train line you ride for no reason. Belonging grows from repetition.”
Player (choice):
“It feels fake to force it.”
“I like the idea of anchors.”
Orson (if 1): “Everything feels fake until it’s familiar. Familiarity is just time you decide to spend.”
Orson (if 2): “Good. Anchors don’t stop the tide; they stop you from drifting.”
Orson (after a beat): “People come here to be seen without being recognized. Tonight I’ll ask you one thing: what surprised you most about Japan so far. Good or bad?”
[The choice doesn’t change the scene; Bearli listens fully, then answers with a soft:]
Orson: “Keep noticing. Noticing is the first kindness we give a place.”
[He raises his glass slightly; the rain keeps time.]
[Scene fades.]
