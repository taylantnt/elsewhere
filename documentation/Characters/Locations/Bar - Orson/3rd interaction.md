[A busier night. The bar’s low light glows against rows of bottles. The player slides into the now-familiar bench. Orson nods, already reaching for the citrus.]
Orson: “Regular’s posture. You sit like the seat knows you.”
(sets the first drink)
Orson: “I trade drinks for stories. Want some?”
Player (shrugs): “Go on.”
Orson: “There was a tourist couple, arguing about the map like it was the last lifeboat on a sinking ship. I gave them two paper cranes and asked them to fold a third together. They kept the cranes, stopped fighting, and sent me a postcard from Kyoto.”
(shrugs)
Orson: “People don’t always need answers. Sometimes they just need a small task to remember they’re on the same team.”
Player: “And locals?”
Orson: “A poet who hasn’t published. A barista who hates coffee but loves steam. A nurse who laughs too loud because the hospital is too quiet. Japan is… polite, yes. But under that, it’s a mess, tender, stubborn, funny. Like everywhere else, just wearing formal shoes.”
Player (small laugh): “That… helps.”
Orson (leans in): “Carry a tiny notebook. Write down three moments each day that felt real: a dog in a raincoat, the station melody, an old man feeding crows. When the city feels too big, your pages make it small again.”
Player (choice):
“I’ll try that.”
“I’ll just live it.”
Orson (if 1): “Good. Memory needs a home.”
Orson (if 2): “Then live it—but don’t forget it. Same difference, different discipline.”

[He slides over a small bowl of edamame.]
Orson: “Eat. Drinking without eating is like sightseeing without looking up.”
[The player cracks a pod open; the bar’s noise swells and softens like a tide.]
[Scene fades.]
