[The room is nearly empty today. Two other readers, both lost in their own books. The blinds are open for once, and the late sun lays a long bright stripe across the floor, right down the middle of the room.]

[Narration]: She's standing at the far end of that stripe. And she's looking at me.

[Actually looking. Not glancing. For the first time, Hana is holding my gaze — and she's holding something else, too. A large, heavy book, clutched against her chest with both arms, cover turned outward.]

[Narration]: I can read the title from here. "Impressionist Masters."

[She takes one step. Then another. Halfway across the room — right at the edge of the sunlight — she stops.]

[Her grip tightens. Her knuckles go pale against the spine. She looks at me, then at the book, then at the floor. Her mouth moves, but nothing comes out.]

[Then she hugs the book to her chest like it's the only warm thing in the building, turns, and retreats behind the shelves. Quiet. Fast. Certain.]

[Narration]: She made it exactly as far as the light reaches. Not one step further.

[I sat there with my heart going. Because I understood something, suddenly. She wasn't trying to hand me a book. She was trying to hand me the thing she hides. And she got all the way to the edge of it and her courage ran out.]

[The book is still on the shelf where she took it from. I know, because there's a gap in the row.]

Player (choice):
"Get up and pull a matching art book off the shelf."
"Stay where I am. Don't spook her."

Player (if 1): [I walk to the art section, slow as I can. I put my hand on a book from the same row — another volume on the Impressionists — and take it back to my seat. I don't look toward the shelves. I just open it, out in the open, where she can see. I read for the rest of the hour with a painting of a water garden on the cover.]

Player (if 2): [I don't move. If she's watching, I want her to see me stay. Staying is the only thing I have that costs nothing and says everything.]

[Somewhere behind the shelves, a page turns. Then another. She's still here. She didn't leave the room this time.]

[Narration]: She's reading. Just on the other side of the row. Our books back to back across a wall of paper.

[The hour ends. When I stand to leave, the gap in the art shelf is filled. She reshelved it — but not in its old place. She put it right at the end of the row, where I'd have to walk past it. Right where someone sitting in my chair would see it on the way out.]

[Scene fades.]
