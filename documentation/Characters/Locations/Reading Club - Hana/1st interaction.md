[The Reading Club meets in a narrow room above a stationery shop. Low wooden tables, mismatched chairs, one long window with the blinds half drawn. The air smells like paper and green tea. A handful of students are already seated, reading. Everyone is quiet. Somewhere near the back, a ceiling fan ticks.)

[Narration / Player's Monologue]: I almost turned around at the door. It felt like walking into a library where everyone already knew the rules and I hadn't been given the manual.

[I hover by the entrance, holding my coat like a shield. A student looks up from the nearest table and waves me over with a small smile.]

Student: "First time? Don't worry, it's not a club-club. No forms, no speeches."

Student: "Just pick any book off the shelves and sit down. We read for an hour. Sometimes people share a thought when the hour ends. Sometimes nobody says anything at all. Both are fine."

Player (choice):
"Can I read in Japanese… even if I'm slow?"
"I don't really know what to pick."

Student (if 1): "Slow is allowed. This is the one room in the city where nobody will rush you."
Student (if 2): "Then pick the shortest one. Nobody's grading you. That's the whole point of this place."

[I sit. Paper rustles. Someone turns a page. I start to relax into the quiet.]

[And then I see her.]

[A girl is shelving returns at the far wall. Dark hair, plain clothes, moving so carefully she barely makes a sound. She slots a book into place, then looks up — and for one split second, our eyes meet.]

[The moment I register her, she looks away. A book slips in her hands. She catches it, tucks her chin down, and walks quickly out of the room through the side door, like the air between us had turned to glass.]

[Narration]: Gone. Just like that.

[I lean toward the student, keeping my voice low.]

Player (whispered): "Who's that?"

Student (shrugs): "Oh… Hana? She volunteers here. Shelves the returns, straightens everything, never stays long."

Student: "Doesn't really… talk. Every time someone tries, she runs off. But she's always around. Every single week."

Player: "Has she ever said anything to anyone?"

Student (a small, unbothered laugh): "Not to me."

[The hour ends. People begin to gather their things. I pull my jacket back on and turn to leave —]

[— and there it is. On my table, a book I never picked up. A slim, older volume, placed square to the edge, spine facing me. I look toward the side door, but there's no one there.]

[The ceiling fan ticks. Somewhere, a page turns.]

[Narration]: I don't know her. I don't know why she left a book on my desk. I only know she didn't want me to see her do it.

[Scene fades.]
