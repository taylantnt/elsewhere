[Rain outside. The club room feels smaller and warmer for it — coats over chair backs, umbrellas leaning by the door. The window is a blur of grey light. Everyone reads a little slower on days like this.]

[Narration]: Three weeks now. The book she left is on my shelf at home. I still haven't figured out how to say thank you in a way she'd let me finish.

[I'm actually reading this time. Deep enough that I don't notice her until she's already there.]

[A shadow. She's standing at the end of my table, holding a book in both hands like it might evaporate. Her mouth opens.]

Hana (barely above a whisper): "…Um."

[Narration]: That's it. One sound. So small I'm not even sure it happened.

Player: "…What? Sorry, I didn't catch that."

[Her whole body flinches, like I'd shouted. She bows — too fast, too deep — and backs away, nearly knocking a chair. Then she's gone through the side door, and the only evidence she was ever here is the chair still rocking.]

[Narration]: I shouldn't have said anything. Or — I don't know. I didn't do anything wrong. So why does it feel like something broke?

[The student next to me chuckles softly, without looking up from the page.]

Student: "Told you. Impossible to talk to. Don't take it personally — everyone tries once."

Player: "She almost said something."

Student: "She does that. Every few weeks, some new person, some new half-word. Then she bails. It's like she wants to, and then… her body vetoes her."

[I stare at the side door.]

[Narration]: Almost. She almost said something. That has to mean something, right? You don't try four times with someone you want to forget.

[The hour ends. I pack up slowly. As I reach for my coat, I notice something on the floor by my chair — a thin corner of paper, half-tucked under the leg of the table. I pull it free.]

[A torn corner of a page. On it, in careful pencil: half a drawing of a window with rain on it. Finished right up to the last two raindrops. Then abandoned.]

[I fold it and put it in my pocket.]

[Scene fades.]
