# Character Profile: Aya (Maid Cafe)

## Snapshot

| Field | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Maid at an Akihabara-style café; "Aya" is her maid name and she is the café's English-speaking host for foreign guests |
| **Location** | Maid Cafe |
| **Archetype** | The Hidden Dreamer / The Soft Goodbye |
| **Affection Variable** | `aya_affection` |
| **Language** | Conversational English, learned on her own — that's why she was chosen to serve foreign customers |
| **Confidant Theme** | Courage, performing a role, and dropping the mask |
| **Leaves / Closes?** | **Yes — she quits the café. The café stays open, but she is gone.** |

## Personality
- **Surface**: High-energy, practised "moe moe kyun" cheerfulness, an ideal maid persona.
- **Deep**: Thoughtful, tired of performing, creatively ambitious and frightened of failing. She is aware that she has been wearing a costume every day for years.

## Appearance & Design
- Maid uniform in early interactions; her own designs / hand-made details appear as her arc progresses.
- Pink hair is her most recognisable trait — and the thing she stops hiding behind.
- The replacement in the epilogue stays **unnamed** ("Maid" in the name box). The point is that the role is filled and the person is not. A portrait can be wired to `new_maid` later without changing any of her lines; it should read as a different girl in the same uniform, cheerfully reciting Aya's notes.

## Backstory
Aya studied abroad briefly (Canada or Australia) and loved the freedom of it. Coming home reminded her of how much pressure there is to fit into a prescribed role. Her parents consider the café job frivolous, and she has never told them what she actually wants. She quietly tests her dreams in the margins of her life.

## Confidant Arc (Interactions 1–8)
| # | Beat |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | First visit. The maid-café concept confuses the protagonist; she carries the whole interaction. "Please come back!" |
| 2 | She remembers him. Reveals she taught herself English. Asks what surprised him most about Japan. |
| 3 | She sits at the table (breaking café rules) and admits how tiring it is to be cheerful on command — and that she once wanted to perform. |
| 4 | Cultural exchange. She talks about studying abroad, and about uniforms, roles and smiles "like masks." |
| 5 | Vulnerability. She's thinking about quitting; her parents disapprove. "When I talk with you, I feel a little braver." |
| 6 | Resolve. She auditioned for something — applying alone made her feel alive. |
| 7 | **Farewell.** She says this may be the last time. She drops the persona and gives her real name: **Shiori**. |
| 8 | **Epilogue: the same omurice.** The café is open and a new maid serves the usual table. Aya trained her from notes, so the cat, the spell and the bow are all correct. The routine is identical and the warmth is not. Repeats on every later visit. |

## How Her Arc Ends (The Departure Theme)
She chooses the unsafe thing. She leaves the café to chase her dream, and the protagonist cannot follow her into that next life.

**In-game consequence**: after Interaction 7 the Maid Cafe can still be visited, but **no one there treats the protagonist the way Aya did**. The room is the same, the routine is the same, the warmth is absent. `aya_affection` is locked. The eighth visit is the repeatable epilogue with the new maid.

## What She Symbolises in the Simulation
Aya is the first argument for leaving the comfortable version of your life. Her line — *"staying here forever would've been safe, and I don't want to be safe anymore"* — is the thesis statement for the "Escape" ending.

## Continuity Note
The performance and the clothing are the same dream, not two competing ones.
Interactions 3 and 6 establish that she wants to sing on a stage; the sketchbook
in Interaction 6 and her reason for leaving in Interaction 7 establish that she
designs the clothes she would wear there. When she leaves, she is chasing both
at once.

## Signature Lines
- "I don't want to be safe anymore."
- "Here, we wear uniforms, roles, even smiles like masks. Sometimes I wonder if people ever see the real me."
- "You let me be myself when I wasn't even sure I could."
- "Thank you for everything. Maybe… someday, somewhere, we'll meet again."
