# Character Profile: Hana (Reading Club)

## Snapshot

| Field | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Volunteer student librarian at the Reading Club; secretly an artist |
| **Location** | Reading Club — a small room off the university library corridor (a quiet room, one hour of reading, optional sharing) |
| **Archetype** | The Quiet Observer / The Girl Made of Held Breath |
| **Affection Variable** | `hana_affection` |
| **Language** | Almost none aloud at first; short WIRE messages and hesitant fragments when she finally speaks (ellipses, pauses, struggling to form sentences). Occasional Japanese slips through when emotional. |
| **Confidant Theme** | Shyness as a cage, art as a voice, and the courage to speak first |
| **Leaves / Closes?** | **Yes — she stops coming to the club to pursue art.** |

## Personality
- **Surface**: A ghost. She shelves books, avoids eye contact, and flees the instant anyone tries to talk to her.
- **Deep**: Not cold — *terrified*. She wants to be seen and is convinced that being seen means being judged. She has a full, vivid inner life that has never once made it out of her mouth. Every small act of approach costs her enormous effort.

## Appearance & Design
- Plain, understated clothes; a club **name badge** that reads *Hana* and a handmade WIRE card she drops when flustered — together they are how the protagonist learns her name and adds her at first.
- Always carrying something to hide behind: a book, a stack of returns, an art book hugged to her chest.
- **`A Child's Garden of Verses` is her book, not a loan.** The flyleaf dedication — *"when the house gets too loud, read this and remember you are allowed to be small"* — is why she keeps it with her.
- The earned-contact object is a **handmade WIRE card**: *Hana — volunteer, student library desk. Reading club. @hana.* It slides out of the book when she panics.
- **`hana.png` is not in the assets folder yet** — she needs a portrait before her interactions can be scripted.

## Backstory
Her parents are strict lawyers who have already decided what her life is going to be. Art is the one thing that is hers, and so it is the thing she hides. She volunteers at the club because it is a room where nobody expects her to perform, and reads art books there in secret. Her grandmother's poetry book is the permission she carries with her: *when the house gets too loud… you are allowed to be small.* She has no friends. She hates that about herself and cannot seem to fix it.

## Confidant Arc (Interactions 1–8)
| # | Beat |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The protagonist notices a girl shelving books. She meets his eyes once, then flees — and later leaves her own book on his table. When he tries to return it, she panics, drops it, and a handmade WIRE card slides out. That card is how he adds her. |
| 2 | He brings the book back. The flyleaf dedication — *allowed to be small* — explains why she carries it. She takes it with both hands and bows instead of running. |
| 3 | She tries to speak, produces an inaudible whisper, panics and runs. |
| 4 | She carries *Impressionist Masters* halfway across the room, freezes, hugs it, retreats. The protagonist may pick up a similar art book in answer. |
| 5 | She sits at the same table, pretending to read, peeking over the top of her book. A folded note is left behind: *"Thank you… for not giving up on me."* |
| 6 | **Confession.** Fragments, slowly: no friends, self-hatred, secret art books, lawyer parents, feeling trapped. She shows her sketchbook — real, raw talent. |
| 7 | **Farewell.** She told her parents the truth. They were angry that she had hidden it, then apologised. She will pursue art. She gives the protagonist a handmade charm: a tiny cloth strap with a brush and palette stitched in thread. |
| 8 | **The Empty Room.** The reading club is still there, but Hana is gone. A different volunteer shelves the returns. The protagonist keeps the charm and sends her one last WIRE message. |

## How Her Arc Ends (The Departure Theme)
Hana's ending is the gentlest version of the same pattern: she grows enough to stop hiding, and the moment she does, the club stops being her hiding place. She stops volunteering and pours her time into her art.

**In-game consequence**: after Interaction 7 she stops coming; the Reading Club is still visitable (it is a public space) but **Hana is not there**. The eighth visit is the empty room: shelves, chairs, the side character, and quiet. `hana_affection` is locked. Her charm stays in the inventory.

## What She Symbolises in the Simulation
Hana is the sharpest small clue that the world is watching. She is the only minor character who seems to exist at the *edge* of the script — orbiting the protagonist, repeating near-identical behaviours, never quite able to speak her line. If the team ever wants a low-key glitch, Hana is the right person for it: a person who behaves like a character who knows she is being observed.

*(Use sparingly. Her story works best played straight, as a genuine shy girl — the reading is left to the player.)*
