# Character Profile: The Ramen Chef

## Snapshot

| Field | Detail |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Role** | Owner and sole cook of a narrow neighbourhood ramen shop |
| **Location** | Ramen Shop (steam, a blue noren, four seats and a counter) |
| **Archetype** | The Gruff Mentor / The Gatekeeper of Tradition |
| **Affection Variable** | `chef_affection` |
| **Language** | Japanese-dominant. He drops a few English loanwords into a sentence and writes the closing notice himself in rough English, but there is no on-screen translator: the protagonist reads his gestures, the bowls, and the handful of words he slowly picks up. |
| **Confidant Theme** | Tradition, patience, and being accepted as a regular |
| **Leaves / Closes?** | **Yes — the shop closes.** |

## Personality
- **Surface**: Gruff, sparing with words, barely makes eye contact. He barks orders at a kitchen that is only himself.
- **Deep**: A craftsman who measures people by whether they come back. He believes food is the shortest bridge between two cultures, and he tests newcomers by serving them whatever *he* decides is right rather than what they pointed at.

## Appearance & Design
- Middle-aged, simple apron, a towel tied around his head, permanently framed by steam and simmering pots.
- His hands do most of the talking: a ladle against the grill, a finger pushing a bowl half a centimetre, two fingers held up for "two days."

## Backstory
He has been making ramen for thirty years and still says he does not understand it. His recipe notebook has a page dated twenty years ago with a simpler recipe; he changes the broth a little every day because "the same is boring." There is no family recipe or wall photograph in the shipped arc. He is alone in the shop until Kenta arrives as his apprentice in Visit 6, and he closes permanently to return to his parents' home in Visit 7.

## Confidant Arc (Interactions 1–8)
| # | Beat |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | The protagonist orders by English, pointing or recommendation and gets a dark pork-and-shoyu bowl he did not expect. He eats it, pays 900 yen, learns うまい (*umai*) if he asks, and gets the chef's WIRE card. |
| 2 | Recognition: the same bowl, 「前、うまかった？」 ("Delicious last time?"), tonkotsu, and the rule 「休むのも、仕事だ」 ("Resting is work too"). |
| 3 | Foreign students struggle with the menu. The protagonist helps, watches, or asks Chef to recommend; Chef rewards the moment with a special bowl and a soft-boiled egg. |
| 4 | CLOSED FOR TRAINING. Over the recipe notebook: thirty years, three characters (味・香・色), and no ramen served that day. |
| 5 | The shop at its busiest. A woman in a suit comes in for the first time, and the protagonist sees the cost of the rush and hears what keeps him going. |
| 6 | Kenta, the apprentice, is introduced. Chef teaches by making a bowl in silence; the lesson is watching, not writing everything down. |
| 7 | **Farewell.** Kenta cooks the test bowl. Chef gives the protagonist his own dark-wood chopsticks marked 心, then announces that he is closing the shop and going home to his parents. |
| 8 | **Epilogue: the closed shop.** The noren is gone and a printed notice is taped to the glass. He has gone home to his parents. Repeats on every later visit. |

## How His Arc Ends (The Departure Theme)
His arc line from the start — you get close to someone, and then they go — lands hardest here because there is no villain and no fix. He leaves Tokyo to care for his parents, and the shop does not reopen. The protagonist's last bowl is Kenta's work, not a ceremonial bowl Chef makes himself.

**In-game consequence**: after Interaction 7 the Ramen Shop is dark and permanently closed. The eighth visit is the repeatable epilogue at the shuttered shop, and the row stays on the hub. `chef_affection` gains one point on the first epilogue pass and is locked after that. The chopsticks are a narrative keepsake, not an inventory item; picking the "Write him a message" choice in the epilogue sends a real WIRE message.

## What He Symbolises in the Simulation
The gatekeeper of assimilation. The simulation is testing whether the subject can earn their way into a closed society. The Chef is the most literal version of that test: harsh at first, generous only to those who persist — and then he is removed, because the simulation's thesis is that belonging is always temporary.

## Signature Lines
- 「いらっしゃい。」 (*Irasshai.* / Welcome.)
- 「どうぞ。」 (*Dōzo.* / Here you go.)
- 「うまい、だろ？」 (*Umai, daro?* / Tasty, right?)
- 「水曜、休み。」 (*Suiyō, yasumi.* / Wednesday, closed.)
- 「休むのも、仕事だ。」 (*Yasumu no mo, shigoto da.* / Resting is work too.)
- 「お前の道を、行け。」 (*Omae no michi o, ike.* / Go your own way.)
