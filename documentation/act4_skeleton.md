# Act 4: Ending Skeleton

**Status: proposal, not canon or playable.** The build ends after Day 69.
Act 3 runs through Day 83. Act 4 is planned for Days 84-90, June 30 through
July 6, 2026. Save the full reveal for that final week.
This draft explores alternatives to the endings in the
[story treatment](story_treatment.md). No ending rules or thresholds are
implemented.

## What the project measures

The facility calls its metric **consolidation**. It believes that someone has
successfully assimilated if they can resume the assigned life after it is
disrupted: answer to the expected role, return to the expected places, and
continue the routine after someone disappears. The percentage is the lab's
interpretation, not a measure of how well a person belongs in Japan. A subject
can look like a success on the monitor while losing the ability to say no.

If that distinction survives the first draft, track a few **named decisions**
in save state instead of accumulating secret points. Early decisions such as
being honest about dinner should echo in dialogue, not secretly cost the player
an ending. The late decisions below should have immediate, readable consequences:

| Decision | What it changes |
|---|---|
| Let the exam print an answer or put down your own | The lab's record and whether the protagonist has an answer he can recognise later. |
| Let the system finish somebody's goodbye or wait for them to finish it | What gets remembered when the scene resets. |
| Respond to the outside signal by number, by name, or not at all | What the people beside the bed hear. It does not choose the ending. |
| Request release or choose to remain at the threshold | The protagonist's intent. Never infer it from a stat. |

Use **Insight, Grace, and Nerve** for different ways through the same problem:
notice a reset, get someone to hold the connection, or ask for release directly.
Always provide a baseline route. A completed confidant arc can contribute one
specific gesture, phrase, or goodbye; no all-maxed requirement. Hikari's
relationship changes the intimacy of the scene, not whether the player is
allowed to wake. During implementation, check a sparse route and a
relationship-heavy route before tuning any numeric requirements.

## Sequence

1. **Finish Act 3 first.** Days 64-69 cover the rail, the ghost exam question,
   the first hospital disagreement about a recorded answer, and optionally
   revisiting places through keepsakes. In Days 70-83 let the pair test whether
   anything survives a change they can reproduce, and let Tetsuo and Miharu's
   response make the cost personal. Save the optional fortune page payoff for
   the threshold so it is never required.
2. **River talk.** Hikari remembers something she could only know as an author
   of this place. Let her show that first. She can tell him what she suspects
   and where she can take him, but she cannot promise she will be waiting on
   the other side. Give him time to ask about her, his body, and what staying
   would mean. No route is selected here.
3. **Two sides of the same conversation.** While still inside, he hears the
   team arguing through gaps in the scene. The older Hikari helped build it
   and objects when the project treats a request to stop as inconclusive data.
   The in-world Hikari was an accidental fragment. The older Hikari opens a
   channel the fragment can use; she does not upload an obedient replacement
   or dictate what her counterpart must do. Let the fragment decide what she
   says. Tanaka treats stable readings as evidence of success; the older
   Hikari asks him to record what the subject actually said. Keep the
   cashier/doctor recognition in a familiar gesture or cadence.
4. **Threshold.** The player tries the channel before deciding. Make one
   signal come back from outside, such as the same knock answered from the
   white room. State the stakes in plain language, then ask for the final
   choice. Be explicit about any later irreversible step. The protagonist
   does not wake up already facing a menu.

## Route sketches

**Leave.** Play the walk out. Let the player choose what to carry in memory,
or who to speak to if a relationship unlocked that goodbye. He wakes in the
facility, not in a replica of the library. The older Hikari is there and
Tanaka can be heard or briefly seen. Give the player one small action before
the fade: answer to his name, ask who authorised the test, or ask what happened
to the Hikari inside. Don't turn the recovery bed into an exposition scene.

**Stay.** Make it an informed decision and give the player one short,
playable return to a familiar place. They can try the old routine or do one
thing the world never asked them to do. Someone can still answer back, but
not everyone returns on demand. Outside, the facility records a stable session
and no return. Default to a still-living, unresponsive body if the link
continues; decide who keeps the session running and on whose authority. An
optional final image of a new arrival meeting an echo of the protagonist
works as ambiguity, not as proof that he died or that the echo is him.

**Interrupted.** Separate this from choosing to stay. The facility closes the
channel before it has confirmed an answer. Only use this route if the player
has had a clear chance to act and can see the risk of allowing the channel
to reset. Never trigger it because of a low attribute total or unfinished
visits. The file can say the subject failed while the player has just seen
the project fail to listen. Leave his ultimate physical fate unresolved.

## Decisions to leave open

- Is staying a viable long-term choice, or does the facility leave the session
  running only for an observation window?
- Does the fragment persist after release? Ask this at the bedside before
  deciding how much of an answer the game should give.
- Does the stay coda show a re-used echo, or is one changed breakfast the
  stronger final image? Brain death would be a separate, much darker canon
  decision, not shorthand for being unresponsive.
- Do we need an interrupted route at all? Keep it only if the scene makes
  losing the choice meaningfully different from refusing to leave.

**Next playable slice:** follow up on Day 69's experiment. Use a few short
hospital fragments across Act 3, then one clear hospital scene near the end
of the act to show what the protagonist did not hear. Keep the river reveal
and the ending choice in Days 84-90.
