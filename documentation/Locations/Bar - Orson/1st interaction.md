[Approach: Saturday evening after the library. The map has stopped helping; every street looks like the last one until the street gives up entirely. The player does not know where they are — only that this is not the way home. The shops are closed early, the signs are tired, and the lights are the same pale blue everywhere. Then there is a shop that does not belong to the street at all — no menu board, no vending machine, no students deciding whether to hurry. Only a low door with a cloth over it and a small wolf painted on the sign beside it. The paint is faded but deliberate.]

[Narration / Player's Monologue]: I mean to look. Only look. Maybe until I can admit I am lost. Curiosity does not wait for permission. By the time I blink, the cloth is behind me and I am inside.

[Setting: A narrow, dimly lit bar with warm amber lights reflecting on polished wood. Soft jazz hums low. Patrons sit in quiet clusters along the counter — a fox, a rabbit, a plain white face — all in partial masks, none of them talking to each other. The air smells of citrus peel and whiskey. Behind the counter, a wolf in a half-mask is polishing a glass.]

[The Master doesn't look up when the player takes the stool at the end of the counter. The only spot that fits without bumping shoulders. The stool wobbles.]

[Master]: "…"

[Narration]: That is the whole greeting. He goes back to the glass. Somewhere down the bar the rabbit laughs at nothing, too loudly, and nobody else reacts — like it happens every night. After a while he sets a glass of water in front of the player. The player didn't order anything. There is no menu they can read.

[Narration]: They sit there long enough to feel foolish. Every instinct says turn around now.

[Master]: "Easy. You look lost."

[Narration]: It comes out in fluent English — unhurried and warm, like he has said it a hundred times to people who needed to hear it. The player had braced for Japanese, or for silence. Not for this. The wolf does not look up while he says it, like the words are only there to keep them from leaving.

[Master]: "Curiosity is allowed in here. So is being lost. You don't have to know where you belong yet."

[Player's choices]
"…Thanks."
(Say nothing. Drink the water.)

[Narration]: Either way, he notices. The wolf mask tilts — barely — in what might be a nod. Then he says:

[Master]: "You found us. Most people don't."

[Narration]: Not "welcome." Not "what'll it be." Just that — like he's confirming something he already suspected. The silence settles back into place around it, but it feels less like a wall now.

[Two of the masked regulars finish their drinks and leave without a word. The third — the fox — catches the player's eye for half a second and looks away first, which somehow feels like the polite thing to do here.]

[Before the player leaves, the Master says one more thing, still not looking up:]

[Master]: "The water's on the house. The conversation isn't. But you're welcome to it."

[Narration]: He says it like the door is staying open, not like a test. On the way out there is a small wooden bowl on the counter with a card beside it — something about leaving a note for the Master. The player doesn't use it. But they remember it exists. Like a door they could knock on next time. Beside it is a small black card with a square WIRE code printed on it.

[Master]: "If you come back, this is how you knock."

[Player]: "That's a QR code?"

[Master]: "It's a door with better manners."

[Narration]: They don't know what to call the feeling by the time they reach the street. It is not relief exactly. It is closer to being let in somewhere.

[Narration]: On the walk home, the player takes the black card out under the streetlight, opens WIRE, and holds the square code under the camera. Orson's name settles onto the list next to Hikari's. They go home.

(Scene fades with the low hum of jazz and the clink of glass on wood.)
