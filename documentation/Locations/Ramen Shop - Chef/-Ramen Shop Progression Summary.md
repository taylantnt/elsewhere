Progression Summary
    •    1: Awkward first order, an unexpected pork-and-shoyu bowl, 900 yen, and the WIRE card. Umai if he asks what the chef said.
    •    2: Recognition, tonkotsu, and the rule that resting is also work.
    •    3: Foreign students at the counter; a special bowl with a soft-boiled egg follows however the player handles them.
    •    4: Closed for training. The recipe notebook, thirty years, and no bowl served.
    •    5: The shop at its busiest, a woman in a suit, and the cost of the rush.
    •    6: Kenta the apprentice arrives; the lesson is watching, not writing everything down.
    •    7: Kenta's test bowl, the chef's own chopsticks, and the announcement that the shop will close.
    •    8: The permanently closed shop; repeatable epilogue.



Encounter 1 — First Visit
    •    The shop is found by smell: broth and fat hitting a hot pan behind a noren.
    •    The menu is unreadable. He orders in English, points at the second line, or asks for a recommendation; the chef steers all three roads to the same bowl.
    •    The bowl is darker than expected: almost-black shoyu broth, pork, and a hill of green onions.
    •    He eats, gets broth on the counter, and pays 900 yen from the thousand he put down.
    •    He learns うまい (*umai*, delicious) if he asks what the chef muttered, and leaves with the chef's WIRE card and a queued shop advert.
    •    Tone: awkward but comforting; food does the first heavy lifting.

Encounter 2 — Recognition
    •    The chef knows his step and points him at the same line as last time.
    •    「前、うまかった？」 checks whether the first bowl landed; the protagonist answers with the one word he knows.
    •    Today's bowl is tonkotsu: lighter, firmer noodles. The menu choice is asking what is being made, watching him work, or thanking him for the last bowl.
    •    「水曜、休み。」 and 「休むのも、仕事だ。」 teach the shop's closed day and the man's rule about rest.
    •    Tone: playful, patient bridge-building without a translator.

Encounter 3 — The Students and the Egg
    •    The shop is full and the chef offers the corner table instead of the counter.
    •    Two foreign students struggle with the menu. The protagonist can tell them the house special, leave them to it, or ask Chef to recommend.
    •    After the rush, Chef brings a special bowl with a soft-boiled egg. His follow-up line and his WIRE message depend on which of the three branches the player took.
    •    Tone: good-humoured; the language barrier is something the room lives with, not a problem to solve.

Encounter 4 — The Notebook
    •    The sign says CLOSED FOR TRAINING, but the door is unlocked and Chef is at the counter with a notebook.
    •    A page dated twenty years ago shows a simpler recipe. Chef changes the broth every day: 「同じじゃ、つまらない。」
    •    Thirty years in, he still says he does not understand it. He writes 味・香・色 — taste, aroma, color — and answers questions about texture, temperature, and perfection by gesture and short Japanese.
    •    No ramen is made today; he tells the protagonist to come tomorrow.
    •    Tone: quiet, precise, and pleased to be understood.

Encounter 5 — The Rush
    •    Word has traveled. Tourists fill the shop and Chef moves faster than the protagonist has ever seen.
    •    The "premium seat" is the counter. A woman in a suit sits beside him, having heard about the place from a coworker; the protagonist can introduce her, leave her to discover it, or tell her what to order.
    •    When the line dissolves Chef admits he is tired, refuses help with the cleaning, and deflects the question of retirement with 「止まったら、死ぬ。」
    •    He brings a bowl at the end of the rush and says it was a good day.
    •    Tone: exhilarating, then quieter; the man is exactly where he is supposed to be.

Encounter 6 — The Apprentice
    •    The shop is unusually empty. Kenta, a young apprentice with a shaky notebook, is introduced by name.
    •    The protagonist can ask how long he has been learning, what he is working on, or tell him it takes time.
    •    Chef makes a bowl without explaining it. The second menu is about watching versus writing everything down; Chef says forgetting is fine.
    •    He sets the finished bowl in front of the protagonist and then asks Kenta whether he saw the most important thing: the customer's face.
    •    Tone: patient and exact; the lesson is not the recipe.

Encounter 7 — The Last Bowl
    •    Kenta, no longer terrified, cooks the usual under Chef's watch. The bowl is good, not perfect; the protagonist's verdict is one of three branches.
    •    Chef gives him his own polished dark-wood chopsticks, marked 心. The meaning can be asked about; otherwise the character stays unread.
    •    After the meal, Chef announces that he is closing the shop and returning to his parents' home. Kenta is told to walk his own road.
    •    The protagonist finishes the bowl in front of him: Kenta's best, and the last one the shop will serve him.
    •    Tone: tender and final, carried by gestures rather than a long explanation.

Epilogue — The Closed Shop
    •    The noren is gone; an English notice is taped to the glass.
    •    The protagonist can leave quietly, send Chef a WIRE message, or sit at the counter. The pots and the recipe notebook are gone; the chopsticks remain.
    •    Bookkeeping runs once. Replaying the epilogue only brings the scene and its choices back.
