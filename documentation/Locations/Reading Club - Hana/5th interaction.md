[Warm, drowsy afternoon light. Fewer readers than usual — just three of us and the ticking fan. The room has that slumped, unhurried feel of a place that knows everyone is tired.]

[Narration]: I take my usual seat and spread out my book, expecting the usual: a book left near my table, a shadow at the edge of my vision, a bow, gone.

[Instead, she sits down.]

[Hana sets a book on the same table. Not near it — on it. She pulls out the chair across from me, sits down stiffly, and opens the book to a random page. She holds it up in front of her face like a shield.]

[Narration]: She's pretending to read. Badly. The book is upside down.

[I don't say anything. I don't think I breathe. I just open my own book and try to keep my eyes on the page.]

[After a while, I feel it — the tiny weight of being watched. I glance up. Over the top edge of her book, her eyes flick away, and the book rises another inch to hide her face.]

[Narration]: She peeked. She peeked at me.

[We sit like that for most of the hour. Two people reading badly. Neither of us turning a page.]

[The hour ends. Chairs scrape. People pack up. Hana stays very still until I start to gather my things — then, in a single quick motion, she stands and heads for the door. She doesn't look at me. She doesn't bow.]

[Narration]: On the table, where her book was, there's a small square of folded paper. Folded four times, edges lined up with obsessive care, sitting dead centre in the space between where we were sitting.]

[I wait until she's gone to pick it up.]

[Inside, in careful pencil, in handwriting that's small enough to fit on a train ticket:]

Hana (written note): "Thank you… for not giving up on me."

[Nothing else. No signature. No smiley. Just those seven words, and the ghost of an eraser mark beneath the final line, like she'd written it more than once before getting it right.]

[Narration]: I read it three times standing by the door. The room's empty now. The fan ticks. Somewhere below, the stationery shop's door chimes as someone comes in.]

[I fold the note back exactly the way she'd folded it, along every crease, and put it in my pocket with the torn window drawing.]

[Scene fades.]
