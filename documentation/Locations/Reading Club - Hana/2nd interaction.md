[Same room. Same hour. The window light is thinner today — late afternoon, everything turning amber. The usual readers are in their usual seats. The ceiling fan ticks its slow clock.]

[Narration]: I came back. I told myself it was for the books.

[I take the same table as last time. The book she left is in my bag, and I still don't know what to do with it. I set it on the table, spine out, in case she's watching.]

[I read. Ten minutes. Twenty. Nothing.]

[And then — a shadow at the edge of my table. She's there, a stack of returns balanced against her chest, so close I can see the loose thread on her sleeve. She sets a book down near mine. Not on my table. Just… near it. Close enough to be meant.]

[She doesn't leave immediately this time. She pauses — half a second, one full second — hovering as if she's going to say something. Then she turns and walks back toward the shelves.]

[Narration]: That's when I see it. Pinned to her collar. A small plastic badge, printed in plain lettering.]

Hana.

[So that's her name.]

Player (calling softly): "Hana… thanks. For the book."

[She stops. Her shoulders go rigid. Slowly — like turning a key in a rusted lock — she half-turns toward me. She doesn't speak. She bows. A quick, deep bow, eyes on the floor, then straightens and disappears into the shelves.]

[Narration]: But she didn't run. She walked. That's… different.

[The student from last week slides into the chair beside me, following my gaze.]

Student (quietly, almost fond): "See? That right there? That was practically a conversation, for her."

Player: "That was a bow."

Student: "Yeah. That's a lot for Hana."

Student (a shrug): "She's like a ghost. Shows up, disappears. Honest? I don't think she has any friends. Not one. People give up on her after a week or two."

[I look at the book she left. Then at the empty space where she was standing.]

[The hour ends. Regulars drift out. I stay a little longer than I need to, turning the book over in my hands. At the very back, just inside the cover, someone has pencilled a small, private star next to the title. Faint. Practically invisible.]

[I put the book in my bag.]

[Scene fades.]
