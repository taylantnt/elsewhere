# Minigame Ideas

## In the current opening

**Day-memory pairs** — The first night turns eight images from the player's
chosen first-day route into a 4×4 matching board. The cards are previewed briefly;
the player wins by finding all pairs before three mismatches. A win grants
0.3 Insight once. A miss changes only the reflection, not the story path.

## Good candidates for later scenes

### Dream: Signal Echo

The dream plays a short sequence of original tones, with a matching light or
shape for each tone. The player repeats the sequence using four on-screen
symbols. Start with three beats and add one beat after each success, up to five.
Offer a replay button and visual cues so the puzzle does not depend on hearing.
Failure should blur the dream rather than block the story; a clean completion
could grant a small, once-only Insight reward.

### Haiku: Find the Season

Show a short poem and ask the player to choose which word acts as its seasonal
image. Explain the answer after the choice. This fits an exam or study scene,
works without a timer, and tests the lesson's kigo idea rather than memorized
syllable counts.

### Photography: Frame the Detail

Present three crops of the same scene and ask which one best preserves a small
clue or emotional detail. Sim can explain what changes between the frames.
Use it to teach observation and character perspective, not as a reflex test.

### Station: Choose the Route

Let the player compare two or three real-feeling routes by transfers, cost, and
arrival time. A correct answer can protect punctuality or money, while a
reasonable alternative still reaches the scene with a different consequence.

## Design guardrails

- Keep minigames short and make their rules clear before the first input.
- Do not make a required story scene depend on perfect performance.
- Award progression once per meaningful scene, not once per replay or attempt.
- Provide non-audio cues and avoid reaction-time requirements unless the scene
  specifically calls for them.
