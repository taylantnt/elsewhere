Progression Summary 
    •    1: Awkward introduction, wrong order, curiosity sparks.
    •    2: Recognition, learns simple word (oishii), playful encouragement.
    •    3: Miscommunication leads to spicy mishap (karai), shared laughter.
    •    4: Hikari appears, translates, reveals family recipe roots.
    •    5: Chef serves the “usual,” gestures at old photo, quiet trust forms.
    •    6: Revelation through Hikari—chef may leave to care for aging parents.
    •    7: Farewell; final ceremonial bowl, keepsake gift (omoide), heartfelt parting.



    Encounter 1 — First Visit
    •    Player finds the shop out of hunger/curiosity; chef speaks only Japanese.
    •    Awkward pointing at the menu → wrong bowl arrives (surprise flavours).
    •    Player tries it, is pleasantly shocked; a first, clumsy bridge forms.
    •    Tone: awkward but comforting; food does the first heavy lifting. 

Encounter 2 — Recognition
    •    Chef recognizes the player and greets them with a nod.
    •    Chef deliberately teaches a simple word (e.g., “oishii” = delicious), repeats it slowly.
    •    Player stumbles through pronunciation; chef laughs warmly and encourages them.
    •    Subtle shift: the shop feels less foreign and more welcoming.
    •    Tone: playful, patient bridge-building.

Encounter 3 — The Language Dance
    •    Communication remains clumsy: chef gestures at ingredients, player guesses.
    •    Mini-mishap (e.g., accidentally ordered spicy miso → player reacts to heat).
    •    Chef laughs, rushes water, repeats a new word (e.g., “karai” = spicy); shared laughter follows.
    •    The miscommunication becomes part of the charm; both relax.
    •    Tone: humour through misfires; mutual good will deepens.

Encounter 4 — Bridging Worlds (Hikari cameo)
    •    Hikari (host sister) appears and casually translates a few lines, easing the flow.
    •    Chef explains (through Hikari) the shop’s roots — a family recipe and years in the neighborhood.
    •    Small ritual introduced (saying itadakimasu, slurping etiquette, tiny local tips).
    •    Player begins to feel like part of the local rhythm, not just a visitor.
    •    Tone: warm, anchored by Hikari’s gentle translation and social glue.

Encounter 5 — Quiet Bond
    •    Chef starts anticipating the player’s preferences; a “usual” is served without asking.
    •    He gestures toward a faded photo on the wall (family, countryside shop) and silently shares a small memory.
    •    Language barrier remains, but silence becomes comfortable—friendship built beyond words.
    •    Chef stamps the player’s card or leaves a small token (paper coaster / tiny freebie) as a soft ritual.
    •    Tone: understated intimacy; trust grows through attentiveness.

Encounter 6 — The Revelation
    •    Hikari joins again and helps translate more seriously this time.
    •    Chef admits (slow, careful words) that his aging parents need him back in his hometown outside Tokyo.
    •    He worries about who will take care of the shop—uncertainty and responsibility surface.
    •    The player witnesses the weight behind the chef’s normally calm demeanor; foreshadowing becomes real.
    •    Tone: bittersweet; change moves from rumor to reality.

Encounter 7 — Farewell
    •    The shop is unusually still; the final bowl is prepared with ritual care.
    •    Chef serves one last, ceremonial bowl to the player; each movement feels intentional.
    •    He gives the player a small keepsake (wooden ramen spoon or engraved chopsticks) and says a simple word like “思い出 / omoide — memory.”
    •    Hikari translates and helps frame the moment, but the emotional meaning is apparent even without full translation.
    •    The goodbye is tender and gutting: gratitude, grief, and a bond that transcends language.
    •    Tone: tender, gut-wrenching, grateful — a lasting emotional echo.
