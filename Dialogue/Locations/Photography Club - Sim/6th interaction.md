The room is warmer than usual. Everyone seems restless but excited — there’s chatter about new projects, prints spread across the table, and even some laughter bouncing between members.
Sim, usually the quiet one, looks… different today. More animated. His camera is slung over his shoulder like always, but his hands can’t stay still — he keeps drumming on the desk.
Sim: “So… something happened. I wasn’t sure if I should even bring it up… but—”
(he pauses, eyes darting, then grins wide)
Sim: “I got an email. From a magazine. They want to feature one of my photos.”
The room erupts.
Member A (Yumi): “No way! Are you serious?! That’s incredible, Sim!”
Member B (Riku): “Finally! Someone out there gets it. We’ve been telling you for ages—your work is amazing.”
Sim: (trying to laugh it off, but voice cracking slightly)
“Yeah, well… it’s just a small thing. A single photo in a spread.”
Yumi: “Still! That’s how it starts. First a spread, then your own feature.”
Sim sits down, finally letting his excitement show.
Sim: “The editor even hinted… if this goes well, they might want me to work with them directly. In another city. It’s… huge.”
Riku: “…Wait. Another city?”
The energy in the room shifts. For the first time, silence falls.
Sim: “Yeah. It’s not confirmed yet, but… I’d have to move. Leave the club. Leave Tokyo.”
Player:
(Encourage) “You have to go for it. This is your dream, right?”


(Worried) “But… what about us? About the club?”


(Curious) “What’s the city like? Do you think you’ll enjoy it there?”


Sim’s Response (varies, but converges)
Sim:
“I don’t know. It scares me. Starting over, leaving everything I finally found here… all of you. But maybe… maybe that’s what growth looks like.”
He looks around the room — at every member — and you feel how much he means it.
Yumi:“Then we need to make the most of the time we do have. Right?”
Riku:“Yeah. Next week’s exhibition. That’s our chance to show everyone what we’ve been working on. To celebrate you, Sim. All of us, together.”
The room slowly regains its warmth. Smiles return. But there’s a heaviness now too — every laugh, every joke, edged with the knowledge that time is running short.
Sim: (quietly, but firmly): “Our exhibition will be the last thing we do together. Let’s make it unforgettable.”

The group begins talking about which photos they’ll submit, voices overlapping, laughter bubbling again. But underneath it all — you feel the weight of what’s coming.
