[Late evening. The bar hums softly; neon leaks in through the frosted window. The player hesitates at the door, then slides onto the narrow bench. Orson, the wolf mask with a warm grin and yellow accents—glances over while polishing a glass.]
Orson: “Back again, hm? That bench didn’t scare you off after all.”
(tilts his head)
Orson: “Where’re you from? Accent’s not from around here.”
Player (choice):
“Say where you are from.”
“A little bit from everywhere.”
Orson (light chuckle): “Everywhere’s a good answer. Tokyo’s made of people from everywhere, pretending they’re from here.”
(sets a coaster with a tiny wolf logo)
Orson: “Anything you don’t want? Sweet, sour, boozy, gentle?”
Player (choice):
“Surprise me.”
“Something light. Still new to this.”
[Orson works unhurriedly. Ice clinks, citrus zest curls into the glass.]
Orson: “You know why the masks? It’s not a gimmick. It’s permission. Some folks need armor to speak freely. Some need to hide to tell the truth.”
(nudges the drink forward)
Orson: “You don’t have to be anyone here. That includes trying too hard to be yourself, if that makes sense.”
Player: “I’m… still figuring out who that is, here.”
Orson: “Good. Don’t rush it. Tokyo’s a marathon with no finish line. First rule for newcomers: keep an eye on the last train. Miss it and this city tests you fast.”
(points toward the wall clock)
Orson: “Second rule: if you can’t say it, write it. A napkin sketch, a translation app, a smile. Communication’s more than words.”
Player (half-smile): “I feel like I’m always one step behind.”
Orson (gentle): “Then take smaller steps. Order the same drink twice. Learn one new phrase a night. Decide which convenience store clerk you’ll always say ‘good evening’ to. We belong to places that notice us back.”
[A pair of masked regulars laugh near the jukebox. Orson lowers his voice.]
Orson: “Also, don’t confuse silence with judgment. In Japan, quiet often means kindness, giving you space not to fail in public.”
(pauses, then lighter)
Orson: “Finish that, and I’ll teach you how to say ‘refill, please’ without sounding like a tourist.”
Player (choice):
“Teach me.”
“Maybe next time.”
Orson (if 1): “おかわり、お願いします。 okawari、 onegaishimasu. Soft on the ‘ri’. You’ll charm half the city.”
Orson (if 2): “Next time it is. Coming back is its own kind of courage.”
[Fade with the soft clink of glass, the player’s shoulders a little less tense than before.]
[Scene fades.]
